## Task: write the DAILY STANDUP

One reader, on their phone, first thing. They want to know **what moved, what is stuck, and what
needs them** — in that order of usefulness, though not necessarily in that order of sentences.

**Length: 3–5 sentences, 90 words maximum.** A standup that runs long stops being read.

Structure it as flowing prose, not a list:

- **Lead with the single most consequential thing** that changed since yesterday. If two things
  compete, pick the one closer to a person using the product; the other gets a clause, not a
  sentence.
- **Say what it means**, not what was done. The evidence pack gives you roadmap movement first
  precisely because that is written in product language — use it. Commits are corroboration, not
  the story.
- **Name anything blocked or waiting on a person.** This is the most useful line in a standup and
  the one most often left out. If something needs the product owner specifically, say so — plainly, with
  no date attached.
- **If nothing meaningful moved, say that in one sentence.** Do not reach for the largest-sounding
  item to fill the space.

The compact signal block posted underneath your prose already carries the actionable pointers —
which branch is red, which PR conflicts, how many checks are owed. **Do not duplicate it.** You are
writing the part a machine cannot: what it meant.
