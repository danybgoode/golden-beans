**Betting** is the process of choosing which projects to schedule for a specific six-week cycle. It replaces traditional backlog management with a high-stakes, deliberate commitment of time and resources to well-shaped pitches.

### Bets, Not Backlogs
The Shape Up method explicitly rejects **backlogs**, viewing them as "big time wasters" that create a feeling of being perpetually behind. Instead of maintaining a central, growing list of tasks, the organization uses **decentralized lists**. Different departments (like Support or Product) track their own requests and bugs independently, but these are not direct inputs to the betting process.

Only well-shaped **pitches**—which have been de-risked and assigned a specific appetite—are considered at the betting table. If a pitch is not chosen, it is let go; if the idea is truly important, it will naturally resurface later when the time is right.

### The Betting Table and Cycle Structure
The organization operates in a repeating rhythm of **six-week cycles** followed by a **two-week cool-down**.
*   **Cycles:** Six weeks is considered the "ideal" length—long enough to ship a meaningful feature but short enough for the team to feel a looming deadline and make necessary trade-offs.
*   **Cool-down:** During these two weeks, there is no scheduled work. Teams fix bugs and explore new ideas, while stakeholders meet at the **betting table** to decide on the next cycle's projects.
*   **Team Allocation:** Bets are placed on either "Big Batch" projects (one project for six weeks) or "Small Batch" projects (a set of 1–2 week projects).

### The Rules of a "Bet"
A bet is treated as a firm commitment, characterized by three main principles:
1.  **Payout:** Each bet is shaped to ensure a meaningful feature is finished by the end of the cycle.
2.  **Uninterrupted Time:** Once a bet is made, the team is granted exclusive focus. They are never pulled away for "quick fixes" or other interruptions, as losing even a single day can destroy their momentum.
3.  **The Circuit Breaker:** This is a core risk-management policy. If a project is not finished within the six-week window, it is cancelled by default rather than extended. This prevents "runaway projects" and forces the organization to reframe the problem or solution if the original approach failed.

### Strategic Betting and Product Modes
Stakeholders at the betting table (typically the CEO, CTO, and senior strategists) evaluate pitches based on the product’s current development phase:
*   **R&D Mode:** For new products, the focus is on "spiking" key pieces to learn what works. Nothing is expected to ship to customers yet.
*   **Production Mode:** Once the architecture is settled, the goal shifts to shipping (merging) finished work at the end of each cycle.
*   **Cleanup Mode:** Prior to a major launch, the structure is loosened to allow a "free-for-all" on must-have fixes and final-cut decisions.

To ensure a bet is sound, stakeholders ask critical questions: **Does the problem actually matter?** Is the **appetite** appropriate? Is the **solution** attractive and the **timing** right? And finally, are the **right people** available to execute it?. The process concludes with a **kick-off message** to the entire company announcing the bets and team assignments for the new cycle.
